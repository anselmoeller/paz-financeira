How to track monthly expenses and plan your budget

Expense tracking works best when you can tell the difference between money already received, money already spent and bills that are still due.

1. Start with income actually received

Record salary and other income when it reaches you. Expected income is useful for planning, but it should not be treated as available cash before it arrives.

2. Group recurring and flexible expenses

Identify regular expenses such as rent, utilities and subscriptions. Track changing costs, including groceries, transport and one-off purchases. Remember to account for future installments.

3. Update the payment status

When you pay a bill, mark it as paid. Keep amounts and due dates accurate: a manual tracker cannot automatically know what happened in your bank account.

4. Review the month before new purchases

Compare received income, paid expenses and remaining commitments. Leave room for unexpected costs rather than relying exclusively on a projected balance.

5. Make time for a weekly review

A short weekly check helps catch forgotten subscriptions, missing entries and bills approaching their due dates. A useful budget is one you can maintain.

Practical habits to start today

  • Use a few meaningful categories instead of dozens at first.
  • Small purchases add up, so record them consistently.
  • Use the available access protection when sharing a device.
